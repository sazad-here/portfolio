❤️ Follow a Patient Down the Risk Tree

Set a patient's blood pressure, cholesterol, smoking and weight. A marker walks them down the decision tree SAS fitted on 5,209 Framingham patients, and the chart shows how the share of deaths from coronary heart disease (CHD) builds at each split.

The tree shows the share of each group's deaths by cause. 61.8% of patients were still alive when the study ended, so read the numbers as relative risk between groups, not as an individual's chance of dying.

What costs the most years?

Average age at death compared with the low-risk cluster (74.4 yrs)
  • Heavy smokers (25.4 cigarettes/day) die about 10.4 years earlier.
  • Hypertensive patients (183 mmHg, 259 mg/dL) die about 1.6 years earlier.
  • Age at death is only recorded for patients who died, so these are conditional averages.
Source: the SAS Visual Statistics analysis (cluster centroids, node rules, node statistics and variable importance panels for the HEART table in SAS Viya for Learners). Only the splits in the fitted tree are drawn; the normal-BP branch was not split further.
